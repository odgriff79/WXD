# Winter Model Bias Research: Atlantic Progression & European Blocking

Research compiled regarding the observed tendency of weather models to show an overly progressive Atlantic and underestimate blocking over Europe and Scandinavia during winter.

### Key Academic Papers - Blocking Underestimation

1. **Blocking and its Response to Climate Change** (Curr Clim Change Rep, 2018) - Comprehensive review showing 30-50% underestimate of Atlantic/European blocking in winter

## Gemini Search Results

### Academic & Research Papers

1. **On the role of moist and dry processes in atmospheric blocking biases in the Euro-Atlantic region** (2025)
   - Explores why models underestimate blocking frequency by up to 80% in winter, specifically looking at how a "zonal shift" in winds affects Rossby wave breaking and the maintenance of blocks.

2. **Upstream Cyclone Influence on the Predictability of Block Onsets** (AMS Journals)
   - A deep dive into why models struggle with the transition from zonal flow to a blocked state, often leading to "forecast busts" for Europe.

3. **Climate model biases in jet streams, blocking and storm tracks resulting from missing orographic drag**
   - Investigates how the representation of mountains (orography) in models can lead to an "overly zonal" North Atlantic storm track and a lack of European blocking events.

4. **The representation of winter Northern Hemisphere atmospheric blocking in ECMWF seasonal prediction systems**
   - Technical analysis of the "chronic underestimation" of blocking in the ECMWF's SEAS5 system compared to reanalysis data.

---

### Technical Bulletins & Professional Discussions

5. **ECMWF: Atmospheric blocking representation in seasonal prediction systems**
   - Official ECMWF newsletter article discussing the persistent underestimation of blocking in Western Europe and the "systematic errors in the atmospheric model" that drive it.

6. **ECMWF: A new product to flag up the risk of cold spells in Europe**
   - Discusses the "NAO-BLO" diagram used to track transitions between zonal/progressive (NAO+) and blocked (BLO/NAO-) regimes, noting the difficulty in capturing these shifts.

7. **Oxford Research Encyclopedia: Atmospheric Blocking in Observation and Models**
   - A comprehensive overview of the theoretical and practical challenges in modeling blocking, including the tendency of models to favor zonal flow.
